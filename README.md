# Is Financial Volatility Intrinsically Rough?

**A Critical Review of Estimation Methods and Empirical Evidence**

A literature review co-authored by Konstantin Iliev and Dimitar Mitov at Delft University of Technology (November 2026).

## Overview

This paper examines whether the roughness observed in financial volatility is an intrinsic property of the underlying stochastic process or an artifact of volatility estimation and measurement error.

Through a comparative review of four influential studies, we investigate different approaches to estimating the Hurst exponent, including log-log regression, quasi-likelihood estimation, generalized method of moments (GMM), and nonparametric roughness estimation.

## Key Findings

- Volatility proxy construction and sampling frequency can significantly affect estimated roughness.
- Error-corrected estimators continue to provide evidence supporting rough volatility under specific modeling assumptions.
- Apparent roughness in realized volatility does not necessarily imply that the underlying instantaneous volatility is intrinsically rough.
